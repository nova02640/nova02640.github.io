I am like a train that has lost its power and has no brakes. Driven not by an engine, but entirely by the heavy weight of family, work, and children, sliding forward along the tracks on nothing but inertia.

I don't need a steering wheel, and there is no so-called destination — as if I no longer need anything at all. The path ahead was determined long ago, and there seems to be nothing left to worry about. It sounds a bit ridiculous, but most of the time I unconsciously avoid asking myself what I truly feel. I only know what should be done, growing more and more like a machine running on a program.

I used to wonder whether this state of being was right, and then I would firmly tell myself: this is right.

I carry the livelihood of an entire family. The sense of security it brings me, along with the boundless loneliness that comes with it, has long outweighed the cost of losing myself. I love my wife, I love my children. Others might call it a happy burden, but to me, it feels more like a curse of happiness.

The emptiness at the bottom of my heart keeps widening, until it numbs me gradually. My way of seeing the world has grown simpler and simpler: all I ask is stability — no extra trouble, please.

Over these ten years, many feelings have been told only to myself. On the surface, I lack nothing, yet there is hardly anything that can truly ignite the joy within me. And so, I keep moving forward.
